# Lunge: Knee Passes Toe / Forward Knee Travel

Front-knee travel past the toes is not automatically dangerous, but excessive,
uncontrolled forward travel combined with the heel lifting off the ground
usually signals the lifter is falling into the position rather than
controlling it, which increases shear load on the front knee.

Look for: front heel lifting, the torso pitching forward to compensate, and
a rushed, uncontrolled descent.

Fixes: shorten the stride slightly, slow the eccentric (lowering) phase, and
cue "sit down between your hips" rather than "step forward and drop."
Reverse lunges are often an easier regression since they reduce forward
momentum into the front knee.
