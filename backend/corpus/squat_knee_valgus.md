# Squat: Knees Caving Inward (Knee Valgus)

Knee valgus during a squat means the knees drift toward the midline instead
of tracking roughly over the toes as the lifter descends. It typically shows
up hardest at or near the bottom of the rep, when load on the joint is
highest.

Common contributing factors: weak hip external rotators and glute medius,
limited ankle dorsiflexion forcing the knees inward to keep the torso
upright, or simply a lack of body awareness under load.

Coaching cues that tend to help: "screw your feet into the floor" (drive
knees outward without moving the feet), "spread the floor apart," or placing
a light resistance band just above the knees during warm-up sets as a
tactile reminder to push against.

Programming fixes: add banded lateral walks, clamshells, or single-leg
glute bridges as accessory work. Reducing load slightly while the cue is
being learned is reasonable and not a step backward.
